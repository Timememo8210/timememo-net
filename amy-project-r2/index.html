Amy Project
行为激活
设计实验

把逻辑、心理学和高质感触觉艺术，组织成一个学生主导的支持性活动礼盒项目：先做三个原型，再用安全、匿名、低风险的反馈验证它是否真的让孩子更容易开始行动、表达和连接。

版本：R2 展示稿 定位：非临床支持性活动设计 更新时间：2026-06-08

核心判断

  • 1先做小：一个孩子愿意打开、愿意完成的一盒。
  • 2再做准：用匿名反馈判断哪个活动、哪个环境更有效。
  • 3最后做大：学校、医院 donation 或 nonprofit 都要建立在安全试点之后。
  • 4底线清楚：不是治疗，不收敏感医疗数据，不绕过家长和专业人员。

Amy Project
Behavioral
Activation Design

A student-led supportive activity-kit project that combines logic, psychology, and high-quality tactile art. The first goal is to build three prototypes, then use safe, anonymous, low-risk feedback to see whether the boxes help teens start small actions, express themselves, and connect.

Version: R2 presentation draft Positioning: non-clinical supportive design Updated: 2026-06-08

Core thesis

  • 1Start small: one box a teen actually wants to open and finish.
  • 2Then get precise: use anonymous feedback to learn which activity and setting work best.
  • 3Then scale: school, hospital donation, or nonprofit paths should follow a safe pilot.
  • 4Keep the boundary: not therapy, no sensitive medical data, no bypassing parents or professionals.

00
执行摘要

先把低落里的“第一步”设计出来。

Amy Project R2 不是把旧页重写成商业计划，而是把想法升级成一个可展示、可试点、可被家长和导师理解的项目框架。核心产品是三款活动礼盒；核心研究问题是不同活动和社交环境怎样影响青少年的开始行动、表达和连接。

3 Boxes独立完成、亲子协作、社区拼接，分别测试三种支持环境。
学习线心理学、逻辑、行为改变、设计和反馈问卷。
产品线从手作原型到高质感礼盒，再到小规模 pilot。
合作线先家庭和学校，再考虑医院 donation 和 nonprofit。
Logic

结构清楚

把手作步骤做成低错误率算法，让孩子不用猜下一步。

Psychology

门槛很低

把行为激活变成一个 5-20 分钟能完成的小行动。

Art

表达安全

用颜色、触感、形状和故事表达，而不是强迫讲话。

一句话：先做一个孩子真的愿意打开的盒子，再用安全反馈判断它是否值得扩大。
01
为什么做

很多孩子不是不想变好，是第一步太重。

低落状态里的困难常常不是“不懂道理”，而是缺少足够小、足够安全、足够可完成的行动入口。Amy 的优势是从孩子视角理解这种入口应该长什么样。

痛点 1

传统材料太像作业

很多心理健康 worksheet 对孩子来说像任务和审查，打开之前已经有压力。

痛点 2

表达不总能靠语言

孩子可能愿意做、愿意画、愿意拼，但不一定愿意马上解释自己的感受。

行为激活

先做小行动，让行动带回一点能量和完成感。

触觉艺术

用丝带、木质、磁吸、纸艺等材料降低进入门槛。

逻辑结构

每一步清楚、可验证、可完成，减少焦虑和放弃。

项目的价值不是宣称“治好抑郁”，而是设计一个更容易开始、更容易完成、更容易被理解的支持性活动入口。

02
三盒系统

同一个目标，三种社交环境。

三款礼盒不是随机产品，而是三个变量：一个人做、两个人做、很多人各自完成后拼接。这样才能看出“环境”本身对开始行动和情绪支持的影响。

01 独立掌控礼盒

真丝丝带玫瑰、植物花艺、低压力折叠。目标：安全感、掌控感、完成感。

02 家庭之桥礼盒

磁吸结构、几何拼图、双人协作和低压力对话卡。目标：共同专注和自然连接。

03 协作网格礼盒

每个人独立完成一个模块，再加入集体墙。目标：低压力归属感和社区连接。

20 x 20 x 20 规则

R2 设计约束

20 分钟

一个阶段可以在 20 分钟内完成，不拖成大工程。

20 个零件

核心零件尽量少，避免打开后就被复杂度压倒。

20 秒第一步

第一步必须一眼看懂，先开始，再慢慢深入。

规则不是为了限制创意，而是为了让低能量状态下的孩子也有机会开始。
03
学习路线

课程不是装饰，是每张活动卡背后的设计依据。

R2 把夏天学习拆成四条线：心理学大图谱、逻辑结构、行为改变、设计反馈。每周至少把一个概念转化成一张 activity card。

Week 1

心理学入门

Yale Intro Psychology，建立行为、记忆、情绪和社交的基础词汇。

Week 2

逻辑推理

Duke Think Again 和 Brilliant Logic，把说明书写成清楚步骤。

Week 3

行为改变

Wesleyan Behavioral Change，学习 action loop、动机和 behavioral activation。

Week 4

认知偏差

Blind spots、attention bias、attribution bias，做 assumption check 卡片。

Ongoing

设计迭代

每学一个概念，转化成一个盒子任务、说明页或问卷问题。

学习目标不是拿一堆证书，而是让每个盒子的每一步都有理由。
04
导师地图

Wyzant 导师要找三类，不是一类。

R2 里的导师不是“替 Amy 做项目”，而是把高级概念拆成适合 8 年级理解、可以转化成设计的课程。

1

心理学 / 神经科学导师

教 neuroplasticity、学习科学、青少年社交连接和情绪支持。

边界：教育辅导，不做诊断或治疗。
2

逻辑 / 系统导师

把手工步骤转成 deductive logic、checklist 和 error-proof algorithm。

输出：清楚说明书和结构化任务卡。
3

统计 / 行为经济导师

教 present bias、choice architecture 和匿名 QR 反馈问卷设计。

只收产品反馈，不收临床数据。

给导师的标准说明：这是 student-led supportive activity-kit design，不是 therapy practice，也不是 medical study。

05
反馈系统

测设计体验，不测医疗结果。

R2 把“问卷调查系统”改成安全的产品反馈系统。它帮助判断盒子有没有被打开、说明是否清楚、完成后孩子是否更愿意继续，而不是判断病情。

06
安全合作

先让可信成年人看懂，再让项目走出去。

R2 的合作顺序是：家庭小试点、学校 counselor / teacher review、合规 donation、再考虑 nonprofit。不要一开始直接进入医院互动或公开 Amy 的个人经历。

学校路线

  • 先让 counselor 或 trusted teacher 看项目定位。
  • 可做 advisory activity、student showcase 或 wellness club。
  • 优点是反馈快、风险低、可以保护隐私。

医院路线

  • 先找 Child Life / donation office，不直接联系病人。
  • 只送全新、可清洁、符合 wish list 的材料。
  • 任何现场活动都需要医院 staff 审核。

隐私

不公开个人经历，除非 Amy 明确愿意且知道后果。

安全

材料无尖锐、无污染、无医疗承诺。

边界

不治疗、不诊断、不做临床研究。

90 天路线图

三天能开始，三个月要有可展示 pilot。

R2 把项目拆成可以执行的节奏：先整理、再打样、再试用、再总结。每一阶段都有交付物。

0-3 天

目标

确定 R2 页面、三盒定义、第一批课程和导师搜索词。

交付

1-2 周

目标

做出每盒一个纸面 / 材料 prototype。

交付

3-6 周

08
打样计划

先做高质感小批量，不急着做公司。

在中国打样的优势是材料和手工供应链丰富，但第一批不要追求复杂包装。最重要的是安全、清楚、可重复和可反馈。

10-20第一批 sample boxes。数量够看出问题，又不会把预算压到太重。
材料丝带、纸艺、磁吸木件、无尖锐工具、独立包装。
说明一张 start card、一张 step card、一张 reflection card。
质量每盒检查缺件、尖锐边缘、说明是否能独立看懂。
Box 01低成本优先

纸艺和丝带适合快速测试说明清晰度。

Box 02结构风险最高

磁吸和双人协作要重点测试安全与耐用。

Box 03展示效果最好

模块墙适合学校展示和社区 pilot。

09
对外讲法

讲成一个清楚的学生项目，不讲成医疗承诺。

R2 的对外叙事要成熟，但不能夸大。最好的说法是：Amy 用自己的兴趣和学习，设计一套支持青少年情绪健康的活动礼盒，并用安全反馈改进。

Executive summary

Design the first step out of feeling stuck.

Amy Project R2 does not turn the original page into a business plan. It upgrades the idea into a report-style project framework that parents, mentors, and school partners can understand. The product is three activity boxes; the research-safe question is how activity type and social setting affect teens' willingness to start, express, and connect.

3 BoxesSolo, parent/teen, and community-grid settings test three different support environments.
Learning trackPsychology, logic, behavior change, design, and feedback surveys.
Product trackFrom craft prototypes to premium boxes and small-scale pilots.
Partnership trackStart with family and school, then consider hospital donation and nonprofit routes.
Logic

Clear structure

Craft steps become low-error algorithms so teens do not have to guess the next step.

Psychology

Low barrier

Behavioral activation becomes a small action that can be finished in 5-20 minutes.

Art

Safe expression

Color, texture, shape, and story allow expression without forcing speech.

One line: build a box teens actually want to open, then use safe feedback to decide whether it deserves to scale.
01
Why this

Many teens do not lack insight. The first step is just too heavy.

When someone feels low, the problem is often not a lack of advice. It is the absence of a small, safe, finishable entry point. Amy's advantage is understanding what that entry point might feel like from a kid's perspective.

Pain point 1

Materials can feel like homework

Many mental-health worksheets feel like assignments or evaluation before a teen even starts.

Pain point 2

Expression is not always verbal

A teen may be willing to make, draw, or assemble before being ready to explain feelings.

Behavioral activation

Start with a small action so action can bring back some energy and completion.

Tactile art

Use ribbon, wood, magnets, paper craft, and texture to lower the barrier.

Logical structure

Every step should be clear, verifiable, and finishable to reduce anxiety and quitting.

The value is not claiming to cure depression. The value is designing a more startable, finishable, understandable support activity.

02
Three-box system

One goal, three social settings.

The boxes are not random products. They test three variables: alone, two-person collaboration, and individual modules joined into a group wall.

01 Solo Mastery Box

Silk ribbon roses, botanical craft, and low-pressure folding. Goal: safety, control, completion.

02 Family Bridge Box

Magnetic structures, geometric puzzles, two-person collaboration, and low-pressure conversation cards. Goal: shared focus and easier connection.

03 Collaborative Grid Box

Each person completes one module, then adds it to a collective wall. Goal: low-pressure belonging and community connection.

20 x 20 x 20 rule

R2 design constraint

20 minutes

One stage can be completed within 20 minutes.

20 pieces

Keep core pieces limited so complexity does not overwhelm the start.

20-second first step

The first step must be understandable immediately.

The rule does not limit creativity. It makes starting possible when energy is low.
03
Learning path

Courses are not decoration. They justify the activity cards.

R2 turns the summer into four learning tracks: psychology overview, logical structure, behavior change, and design feedback. Every week, at least one concept should become an activity card.

Week 1

Psychology foundation

Yale Intro Psychology builds vocabulary for behavior, memory, emotion, and social connection.

Week 2

Logic reasoning

Duke Think Again and Brilliant Logic help turn instructions into clear steps.

Week 3

Behavior change

Wesleyan Behavioral Change teaches action loops, motivation, and behavioral activation.

Week 4

Cognitive bias

Blind spots, attention bias, and attribution bias become assumption-check cards.

Ongoing

Design iteration

Each concept becomes a box task, instruction page, or survey question.

The goal is not collecting certificates. The goal is giving every box step a reason.
04
Mentor map

Use Wyzant for three mentor types, not one.

Mentors do not build the project for Amy. They translate advanced ideas into middle-school-friendly lessons and design decisions.

1

Psychology / neuroscience mentor

Teach neuroplasticity, learning science, adolescent social connection, and emotional support.

Boundary: educational coaching, not diagnosis or treatment.
2

Logic / systems mentor

Turn craft steps into deductive logic, checklists, and error-proof algorithms.

Output: clear instructions and structured task cards.
3

Statistics / behavioral economics mentor

Teach present bias, choice architecture, and anonymous QR feedback design.

Collect product feedback only, not clinical data.

Standard tutor language: this is student-led supportive activity-kit design, not therapy practice or a medical study.

06
Safety partners

Make trusted adults understand it before taking it outside.

R2's sequence is family micro-pilot, school counselor / teacher review, compliant donation, then maybe nonprofit. Do not start with direct hospital interaction or public disclosure of Amy's personal experience.

School route

  • Ask a counselor or trusted teacher to review positioning.
  • Possible forms: advisory activity, student showcase, wellness club.
  • Fast feedback and lower risk while protecting privacy.

Hospital route

  • Start with Child Life / donation office, not patients directly.
  • Donate only new, cleanable, wish-list-compatible materials.
  • Any on-site activity requires hospital staff review.

Privacy

Do not publicize personal experience unless Amy clearly wants that and understands the consequences.

Safety

Materials must avoid sharp edges, contamination, and medical promises.

Boundary

No treatment, diagnosis, or clinical research claims.

08
Prototype plan

Make a high-quality small batch before building an organization.

Manufacturing prototypes in China can help with materials and craft supply chains, but the first batch should not overfocus on packaging complexity. Safety, clarity, repeatability, and feedback matter more.

10-20first sample boxes. Enough to see problems without making budget or logistics too heavy.
MaterialsRibbon, paper craft, magnetic wood pieces, no sharp tools, individual packaging.
InstructionsOne start card, one step card, one reflection card.

Box 02Highest structure risk

Box 03Best showcase effect

The module wall is strong for school showcase and community pilots.

09
Public narrative

Present it as a clear student project, not a medical promise.

The R2 narrative should be mature without overclaiming: Amy is using her interests and learning to design supportive activity boxes for teen well-being, then improving them through safe feedback.

R2 keeps only the sources needed for action: courses, mental-health boundaries, behavioral activation, hospital donation, and nonprofit requirements.

Yale Online · The Science of Well-Being

Yale public-course entry point, useful for happiness habits and psychology experiments.

Coursera · Introduction to Psychology

Paul Bloom's psychology introduction covering perception, memory, decision-making, social behavior, and more.

Duke / Coursera · Think Again

Duke's logic and critical-thinking sequence, useful for informal logic and argument analysis.

Brilliant · Logic

Interactive logic practice that can become a daily puzzle habit; not the same as a university certificate.

Brilliant · Computer Science

Brilliant's CS pathway covers algorithms, data structures, and logic-oriented problem solving.

Open Yale Courses · PSYC 110

Free lecture version, good for previewing whether Amy likes the teacher and course rhythm.

Coursera · Overcoming Cognitive Bias

Madecraft course for common cognitive biases and everyday decision tools.

Yale / Coursera · Everyday Parenting

Alan Kazdin's behavior-change tools course; useful for reinforcement, phrasing, and step design.

Harvard Online · Psychology of Blind Spots

Mahzarin Banaji's course focused on hidden bias and intuitive decision-making.

Wesleyan / Coursera · Creating Behavioral Change

Covers motivational interviewing, behavioral activation, and SMART goals; the page notes it does not replace clinical training.

Harvard DCE · PSYC S-1609

Neuroscience of Learning course page; useful as a Wyzant tutor syllabus target, not as an 8th-grade on-demand course.

Harvard DCE · PHIL S-12

Deductive Logic course page; useful for argument structure, symbolic logic, and formal deduction.

Harvard DCE · ECON S-1915

Neuroscience and Economic Decision-Making course page; the 2026 listing shows on-campus format and prerequisites.

Harvard SSP · Eligibility

Harvard Secondary School Program eligibility page; use this to avoid assuming middle-school direct enrollment.

Harvard EdCast · Crisis of Connection

Research discussion / interview source for adolescent social connection, not a course enrollment page.

Wyzant · Find a Tutor

Wyzant support page for searching/requesting tutors; parent involvement is the right default for a minor.

NIMH · Depression

Foundational material for understanding depression; it helps keep the project from replacing professional treatment.

Kids Mental Health Foundation · Behavioral Activation

Supports small, valuable activities as a direction; specific kit content still needs professional review.

OHSU Doernbecher · Wish List

Portland local children's hospital donation guideline; usually requires new, unused items.

Oregon DOJ · Starting a Charity

If the future path is charity / nonprofit, this is the official Oregon charitable registration entry point.

IRS · Responsible Parties and EIN

An EIN requires a responsible party; when minors are involved, confirm with parents or professionals first.
