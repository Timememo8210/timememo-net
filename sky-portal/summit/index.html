Program 1 of 3

TITAN

Foundation & First AgentsShort term

The ground floor: what AI costs, what it returns, the data it stands on — and the first working agents that start paying the rent from day one.

The stance — you direct, AI executes ▶

Every task starts with a human instruction: you assign, the agent executes, you review. AI is your hands — real leverage, but the initiative stays entirely with you. The next two programs move exactly that line.

The cost model — four views of the same ledger ▶
  • Quarterly adoption ramp — spend and savings modeled as usage actually grows.
  • High-adoption scenario — the stress test: what happens if it works better than planned.
  • Local GPU vs. hosted API economics — the build-vs-buy line, priced honestly.
  • ROI verification — a closed loop that measures returned hours after deployment.
The data thesis — AI amplifies fragmentation ▶

AI does not dissolve fragmented data; it multiplies the cost of it. Every agent that touches a messy landscape produces answers with messy provenance. That is the strategic case for laying the data foundation first — before the shinier layers stack on top of it.

First-wave agents — meetings, knowledge, time ▶

The first agents target the daily drains — where the time goes, and how we get it back:

  • Meeting-preparation agent — briefs you before you walk in: history, open questions, who committed to what.
  • Meeting-record agents — decisions, actions with owners, open questions and risks, verifiable down to the timestamp (the prompt patterns behind them are taught in BASECAMP).
  • Technology-knowledge agents — answer from the group's own accumulated know-how instead of a hundred scattered folders.

The routing rule that frames them all: right channel, right job — meetings for decisions, documents for depth, messages for speed. Agents take over the record-keeping in between.

Program 2 of 3

ATLAS

Yield & Variation IntelligenceMid term

How AI turns the yield-and-variation problem from a hunt through fragmented data into an indexed space where engineers spend their time judging, not gathering.

The story arc — twelve moves from problem to ask ▶

Problem → Proof → Our Move → How It Works → The Method → Where the Time Goes → What We Keep → One Layer Out → Deployment → Benchmarks → The Ask → Summary & Next Steps.

Each move is one slide, one idea, one conclusion-as-title — built so the argument survives even if a reader only sees the headlines.

The stance — AI points, engineers judge ▶

ATLAS treats AI as an indexer over the variation space: it surfaces candidate signals, correlations, and anomalies with traceable evidence. Engineers keep what matters most — the judgment call and the sign-off on every disposition.

The payoff is time reallocation: hours move from data wrangling and cross-referencing to root-cause thinking.

The shift — it works while you sleep ▶

TITAN agents wait for instructions; ATLAS agents watch the systems around the clock. Yield and variation signals that would take weeks to hunt down surface on their own — findings you did not ask for, on your desk by morning.

The teaching turns two-way: you still train it, and it starts showing you patterns you did not know to look for. AI stops being only your hands and becomes your eyes.

What we keep — the non-negotiables ▶
  • Human sign-off on every disposition. No exceptions.
  • Traceable evidence behind every AI-surfaced lead — auditable in seconds.
  • Deployment is phased, benchmarked, and reversible.
Program 3 of 3

SKYLINE

Autonomous Agent PlatformLong term

Where the road leads: a plant where agents are first-class actors — one hero picture, three pillars underneath it.

Hero + three pillars ▶
  • The virtual plant — a living mirror of the line, where questions get asked and tested before any real metal moves.
  • Agent-native platform — workflows designed for agents as first-class actors, with permission boundaries built in.
  • Agents talk to agents — coordination moves machine-to-machine. You set the goal and judge the outcome; agents research the method, execute, and iterate on their own. The hand-holding ends here.

Prerequisite: the connected data layer, opened to agent access — no open data, no SKYLINE.

Design rule: build for launch-time frontier capability, and skip what the models will absorb on their own.

Training Center · Playbook

The Meeting Prompt Ladder

Five camps, one climb: the same transcript, asked better each time. Each camp is a prompt pattern you can run today — and the training ground for TITAN's meeting agents.

CAMP 5Baseline summaryEffect: you stop rereading transcripts.▶

Ask for a faithful summary of what was discussed. The floor, not the goal — but it already ends the era of scrubbing through recordings to remember what happened.

CAMP 6–7Restructured by topicEffect: the meeting becomes a checklist.▶

Reorganize the meeting into Decisions / Actions with owners / Open questions / Risks — and flag any action without an owner as [UNOWNED]. Unowned actions are where follow-through goes to die; naming them is half the cure.

CAMP 8Verifiable outputEffect: every claim audits in seconds.▶

Every item now carries verbatim quotes and timestamps; anything inferred is explicitly marked as inference. Trust is no longer asked for — it is checkable.

CAMP 9History-awareEffect: you see the story, not the snapshot.▶

Run against the meeting series: track how positions shifted across sessions and which questions keep returning unresolved. Recurring unresolved questions are the project's real risk register.

CAMP +1Meeting dynamicsEffect: you sense the room you weren't in.▶

Timestamped signals only, no verdicts: heated exchanges, dropped questions, who went quiet, soft yeses. The AI indexes the moments; reading them is your job. AI is the index — you are the judge.

▲ Each camp is one prompt away from the last. Start at 5; climb when the output earns it.

Training Center · Rung 3 Primer

Model vs. Agent — the one-line version

A model answers. An agent acts.

What turns a model into an agent ▶

An agent is a model plus five things: an identity and rules, a toolbox, data access, permission boundaries, and a defined deliverable. It then runs a closed loop:

goal → plan → act with tools → observe → iterate → deliver.

That loop is the whole magic — and the permission boundaries are the whole safety story. Nothing an agent does should exceed what its human would sign off on.
